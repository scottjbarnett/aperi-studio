Aperi Energy

Air, water, limestone, and renewable electricity.

Renewably produced electricity can be used to turn air, water, and limestone into a safe and ecologically sound replacement for urea, using decades-proven industrial technology.

Public research track

The question

The question is whether this can now be done in New Zealand in a way that is economically, environmentally, and regionally feasible.

Aperi Energy is researching that question through public-facing work on fertiliser sovereignty, renewable ammonia pathways, nitrogen supply risk, and regional resilience.

Public material

The current public material is a broad discussion document on green ammonia and fertiliser sovereignty. Selected applied concept work remains unpublished while under development.

Why it matters

  • New Zealand agriculture depends on secure access to nitrogen fertiliser.
  • Renewable electricity may create new pathways for domestic or regional production.
  • Green ammonia links energy, agriculture, industrial resilience, and regional development.
  • The feasibility question is not only technical. It is economic, environmental, logistical, and institutional.
