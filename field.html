Aperi Field

Ultrasonic sensing, bat behaviour, and renewable-energy monitoring.

Once time, location, and three-dimensional sound maps can be captured together, acoustic data can be post-processed into behavioural maps.

Early hardware concept

The concept

Aperi Field explores whether compact ultrasonic microphone arrays, GPS-linked logging, and AI-assisted analysis could help visualise bat population movement and possibly identify behavioural patterns in individual bats.

One early concept is a compact ultrasonic ambisonic recorder: a four-microphone MEMS array with GPS-linked logging. The aim is to explore whether small field devices could support directional bat detection, movement mapping, and richer ecological monitoring around renewable-energy infrastructure.

Hardware pathway

The current hardware concept builds from:

T5838 ×4 → PCMD3140 → ESP32-S3 → SD card

Possible outputs

  • Time-stamped ultrasonic recordings.
  • GPS-linked bat activity logs.
  • Three-dimensional acoustic direction maps.
  • Post-processed bat movement visualisations.
  • AI-assisted behavioural classification.
  • Possible identification of repeated individual or group patterns, if technically supported by the data.

Frame of reference

Existing devices such as the Alato AR5 Acoustic Recorder provide an important reference point for bat and bird monitoring in New Zealand. Aperi Field is not claiming to replace validated recorders. The question is whether a compact directional ultrasonic array could add useful movement-mapping and visualisation capabilities.

Renewable-energy context

Bat strike risk is one of the ecological concerns associated with wind-energy development. Better local acoustic monitoring could support site assessment, mitigation design, post-installation monitoring, and research into deterrent effectiveness.

AI may eventually help identify repeated behavioural patterns, movement signatures, or possibly individualised call characteristics, but this would require validation against known populations and field data.
